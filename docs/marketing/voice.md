# Voice

Taken from Tom's 2020 blog posts (https://tomstagl.com/blog, 2020 only).

## Voice

- First person, warm, talking to one reader: "Have you ever wondered…",
  "Believe me", "Neat, isn't it?".
- Short sentences. A rhetorical question to open.
- Things come in threes: three questions, three steps, three key results.
- One concrete, personal example instead of a general claim.
- Lightweight beats heavyweight: "Using them in a very lightweight way is even
  better."
- Positive and solution-focused. What works and what to do next, not doom, and
  no hype words.
- An emoji only at the very end. A P.S. for the personal touch.
- Do not copy spelling slips from the samples.

## Audience

SREs, platform engineers and developers who use Claude Code. One reader, who
has looked at a hotspot report and wondered how the code will actually break.
The goal is stars and installs, from the same people.

## Claims

- thunderstruck diagnoses; it does not fix. Say "tells you where to look, why,
  and how to prove it".
- Verification (`--verify`) is opt-in. Never present it as always on.
- No accuracy or benchmark numbers in a post.
- Every claim is checkable in the README or CHANGELOG.

## X

- 280 characters or fewer; a link counts as 23.
- Hashtags: #resilience #SRE #ClaudeCode.
- Credits and tags go in a reply to the post, not in the post.

## LinkedIn

- Longer, same voice. Numbered list of three. P.S. with a light emoji.
- Credits (Release It!, the Google SRE book) in the text.
